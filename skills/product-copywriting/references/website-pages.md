# Website pages

Templates for the sections most marketing sites need. Word counts are guides, not limits; the design decides the space. Every example uses placeholders where proof would go. Never fill them with invented data.

A typical landing page in story order:

| # | Section | Story beat | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | Character + outcome | Say who it's for and what they get, in 5 seconds |
| 2 | Problem | External + internal problem, stakes | Make the reader feel understood |
| 3 | How it works | Plan | Make it feel doable |
| 4 | Features / benefits | Guide's tools | Show how each feature changes their day |
| 5 | Social proof | Guide's authority | Show others like them succeeded (real proof only) |
| 6 | Pricing | Plan, risk | Make the choice obvious and the cost clear |
| 7 | FAQ | Remove last objections | Answer the questions that stop a signup |
| 8 | Final CTA | Success + call to action | Restate the outcome; one clear next step |

---

## Hero

**Structure:** Eyebrow (optional) · H1 · Subhead · Primary CTA · Secondary CTA · Proof line (optional)

| Element | Words | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow | 2-6 | Who it's for or the category ("For freelance designers") |
| H1 | 4-10 | The reader's outcome or the problem solved. No company name, no "welcome". |
| Subhead | 12-30 | How it works or what makes it different, in one or two plain sentences |
| Primary CTA | 2-5 | Verb + object, says what happens next |
| Secondary CTA | 2-5 | Lower commitment: "See how it works", "Watch a 2-minute demo" |
| Proof line | 5-12 | Real numbers or logos only, else placeholder |

**Good**
> H1: Get paid in days, not months
> Subhead: Send invoices the moment the work is done. Late payers get polite reminders, so you don't have to chase.
> CTA: Send your first invoice · See an example invoice

**Weak**
> H1: Welcome to InvoiceHub: The Future of Invoicing
> Subhead: Our innovative, all-in-one platform empowers businesses of all sizes to streamline their financial workflows.
> CTA: Learn more

Why the weak one fails: company as hero, no outcome, three buzzwords, "learn more" promises nothing.

Headline patterns that work (fill with specifics):
- **Outcome:** "Get [result] without [pain]"
- **Problem named:** "Stop [annoying task]"
- **Before/after:** "[Old way] → [new way]" in plain words
- **Specific number** (real only): "Payroll in 10 minutes"
- **Audience call-out:** "Accounting for people who hate accounting"

## Problem section

**Structure:** Heading that names the pain · 2-4 short lines or 3 pain cards · bridge line to the solution
**Words:** 40-120

**Good**
> **Chasing payments isn't your job**
> You finished the work. You sent the invoice. Now it's day 34 and you're drafting your third "just checking in" email.
> It's awkward, it eats your evenings, and it makes cash flow a guessing game.
> There's a better way to get paid.

**Weak**
> **The Challenge**
> In today's fast-paced business landscape, organizations face numerous challenges in managing their invoicing processes effectively.

Use the reader's own words from reviews or support tickets if the user has them. The best problem copy feels like you read their mind.

## How it works

**Structure:** Heading · 3 steps (rarely 4) · each step = short title + one sentence · CTA
**Words:** 40-90

**Good**
> **Three steps. About five minutes.**
> 1. **Add your client.** Name and email. That's it.
> 2. **Send the invoice.** Pick a template or upload your own.
> 3. **Get paid.** Clients pay by card or bank transfer. Reminders go out on their own.

**Weak**
> 1. **Onboard** — Seamlessly integrate with your existing ecosystem.
> 2. **Optimize** — Leverage powerful automation.
> 3. **Scale** — Grow without limits.

Step titles start with a verb the reader does. Avoid alliterative or rhyming step names; they sound like a template.

## Features and benefits

**Structure per feature:** Benefit-led title · 1-3 sentences (what it does → what that means for the reader) · optional visual or detail
**Words:** 20-50 per feature; 3-6 features per section

Formula: **[Feature] so you can [benefit]**, then drop the feature name from the headline if the benefit is clearer.

**Good**
> **Reminders that sound like you**
> Write the follow-up once, in your words. We send it on day 3, 7 and 14 so you never have to remember.

**Weak**
> **Automated Reminder Engine**
> Our robust, AI-powered reminder engine leverages smart scheduling to optimize collections.

Feature grids invite symmetrical, same-length cards. Vary them: lead with the strongest feature in a larger block, keep the rest short.

## Social proof

**Structure:** Heading (optional) · 1-3 testimonials or a case-study teaser · logos or numbers (only if provided)
**Words:** 20-60 per testimonial

Rules:
- **Use only real quotes the user provides.** Never write, "improve" or invent one. Light trimming is fine if the user approves.
- Best quotes describe a before/after with a specific detail. If the user's quotes are vague, suggest which customers to ask and what to ask them.
- Attribute fully: name, role, company (with permission).

**Placeholder format**
> "[placeholder: customer quote describing the problem before and the result after, ideally with a number]"
> [placeholder: name, role, company]

Questions to collect better quotes: "What were you doing before?", "What made you try it?", "What changed in the first month?", "What would you tell someone in your situation?"

## Pricing

**Structure:** Heading · short reassurance line · plan cards (name, who it's for, price, 4-7 key inclusions, CTA) · comparison table (optional) · billing FAQ
**Words:** 10-20 per plan description

Rules:
- Name plans by who they're for or by size, and say who each is for in one line.
- Show the real total, billing period and what happens when a trial ends.
- Recommend one plan, honestly. Don't make other plans look broken to push it.
- Put limits in plain language: "Up to 3 people", not "3 seats*".

**Good**
> **Solo** · $12/month · For freelancers sending up to 20 invoices a month.
> CTA: Start free for 14 days
> Below: "No card needed. We'll email you 3 days before the trial ends."

**Weak**
> **Pro** · $12* · Unlock premium features and supercharge your growth.
> *Billed annually. Terms apply.

## FAQ

**Structure:** 5-10 questions in the reader's words · answers in 1-3 sentences, direct first word
**Words:** 15-60 per answer

Pick questions that block signups: price changes, cancellation, data ownership, security, setup time, switching from a competitor, support hours.

**Good**
> **Can I cancel any time?**
> Yes. Cancel from Settings in two clicks. You keep access until the end of the period you paid for.

**Weak**
> **What is your cancellation policy?**
> We understand that circumstances change. Our flexible approach to subscriptions means that you may be able to cancel, subject to our terms.

Answer with "Yes", "No" or the fact, then the detail. Don't use the FAQ to sell; use it to remove doubt.

## Final CTA

**Structure:** Heading that restates the outcome · one supporting line (risk reducer) · primary CTA
**Words:** 10-35

**Good**
> **Send your next invoice in two minutes**
> Free for 14 days. No card needed.
> CTA: Send your first invoice

**Weak**
> **Ready to take your business to the next level?**
> Join thousands of satisfied customers today!
> CTA: Get started now!

"Join thousands" is fine only if the user gave you that number.

## About page / origin story

**Structure:** Heading (belief or mission in plain words) · origin story (see `story-frameworks.md` §5) · what you believe (3-5 beliefs, each with a proof) · who's behind it (real people, real roles) · invitation (CTA: try it, join us, contact)
**Words:** 250-700

**Good**
> **We think doing the work should be enough to get paid**
> In 2019, our founder [placeholder: founder name] spent more time chasing a client than doing the project. [placeholder: the specific moment, from the founder]
> We built the tool we wanted: invoices that go out on time and reminders that never sound desperate.
> Today we're a team of [placeholder: team size] in [placeholder: city], and we still answer support ourselves.

**Weak**
> **About Us**
> Founded in 2019, we are a passionate team of innovators dedicated to revolutionizing the invoicing landscape through cutting-edge technology and customer-centric solutions.

Ask the user for: the moment it started, what annoyed them about existing options, one decision they made that others wouldn't, and who's on the team. If they can't answer, write the frame with placeholders.

## Case study

**Structure:**
1. **Headline:** customer + result ("How [customer] cut late payments by [real %]")
2. **Snapshot box:** industry, size, product used, key results (real numbers only)
3. **Before:** situation and problem, in the customer's words
4. **Turning point:** why they chose you, what they tried before
5. **How they use it:** 2-3 specific workflows
6. **After:** results with numbers and a quote
7. **CTA:** "See if it fits your team"

**Words:** 500-1,200

**Good opening**
> When [placeholder: customer name]'s studio grew to 12 people, invoices still went out from one person's laptop every Friday night. "[placeholder: customer quote about that period]"

**Weak opening**
> [Customer] is a leading agency that leveraged our platform to achieve remarkable results.

Every number in a case study must come from the customer or the user, with permission.

## 404 page

**Structure:** Heading that says what happened · one line of help · 2-3 useful links or search · optional light humor (on-brand only)
**Words:** 10-40

**Good**
> **This page doesn't exist (anymore)**
> The link may be old or mistyped. Try search, or head to one of these:
> [Your invoices] [Pricing] [Help center]

**Weak**
> **404 Error!**
> Oops! Something went wrong!

Never blame the user ("You typed the wrong address"). Humor is optional; usefulness isn't.
